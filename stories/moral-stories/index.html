Moral stories for children

Short stories that end with a clear, gentle lesson: patience, honesty, teamwork and respect for old things. The lesson is shown through what the characters do, not preached, and each story ends with three questions that help a child put the moral in their own words.

How to read a moral story with a child

Children do not learn a value by being told it. They learn it by watching somebody make a choice and seeing what follows. So read the story first as a story, with all its suspense, and leave the lesson alone until the end. Then ask the three questions printed under it, and let the child say the moral in their own words before you read ours. A child who says "the pot was useful because it was different" has understood far more than one who has heard an adult say it.

Choosing the right one

Pick a story for what is happening in the child's life this week. A child who gives up quickly will see themselves in the mango seed that could not wait. One who has found money or been given too much change will follow Meenu's problem closely. Do not announce why you chose it; children resent a story that has been aimed at them, and enjoy one they have discovered.

One story, several evenings

Children like to hear a good story again, and each telling does new work. The first time, simply read it. The second time, stop before the ending and ask what the character should do. The third time, let the child tell it to you, or to a younger brother or sister. Retelling is where the moral finally becomes their own. For Hindi stories of the same kind, see हिंदी कहानियाँ.
