How to tell a bedtime story

Children do not remember bedtime stories for the plot. They remember your voice, the dark room and the feeling of being safe. You do not need to be an actor. A few simple habits make any story work.

Before you start

  • Same time, same place. A story at the same point every night tells the body that sleep is coming. Brush teeth first, story last.
  • Lower the lights. If you read from a phone, turn down the brightness and use dark mode. The moon button at the top of this site switches it.
  • One story, agreed in advance. "One story and then lights off" avoids the nightly bargaining. Let the child choose which one.

While you tell it

  1. Go slower than feels natural. Bedtime pace is about half your normal speaking speed. Pause at every full stop.
  2. Let your voice fall. Start in a normal voice and get softer through the story. By the last paragraph you should be nearly whispering.
  3. Repeat the sounds. "Chhuk... chhuk... chhuk", "drip, drip, drip": children love to join in, and repeated sounds are calming.
  4. Use the child's name sometimes. "And do you know what Gajju did, Aarav?" brings a wandering mind back.
  5. Do not test at night. Keep the questions at the end of each story for daytime readings. At bedtime, simply stop and say goodnight.

Choosing the right story

AgeWhat worksLength
3 to 5Animals, the moon, repeated lines, no danger2 to 3 minutes
5 to 7A small problem that is solved kindly; funny stories in the daytime3 to 4 minutes
7 to 10Stories about school, friends and fairness that leave something to discuss4 to 5 minutes

If the child asks for the same story every night

That is normal and good. Knowing what comes next is exactly what makes a story restful. Read it again. Change one tiny detail if you are bored, and enjoy being corrected.

Making up your own

Take one thing from the child's day, a puddle, a lost shoe or a neighbour's dog, and give it a small adventure that ends at home, in bed. That is the shape of nearly every bedtime story on this site. The ones you invent will be the ones your child remembers longest.
