Bedtime stories for children

Calm, slow stories for the last ten minutes of the day. Nothing scary happens in them. They are full of soft sounds, night skies and sleepy animals, and they end quietly so that a child can drift off. Dim the screen, or tap "Read aloud" and put the phone down.

What makes a story right for bedtime

A daytime story may be exciting. A bedtime story has the opposite job: to slow a busy mind. The stories in this list are written for that purpose. Nobody is chased, lost or scolded in them. They move at walking pace, they repeat soft sounds, and each one ends with someone safe, warm and falling asleep, which is a quiet suggestion to the listener.

How to read them

  • Lower the lights first, and settle the child in bed before you begin, so that the end of the story is the end of the day.
  • Read more slowly than feels natural, and let your voice grow softer on each page.
  • Leave long pauses. A sleepy child needs time to see the picture in their mind.
  • One story is enough. If the child asks for another, read the same one again, more quietly.
  • Keep the questions for the morning. At night, simply say goodnight.

If you are away from home

Parents who travel, and grandparents in another city, can read these stories over a phone call. The "Read aloud" button on each story page can also read the text, but a familiar voice, even on a speaker, settles a child better than any device.
