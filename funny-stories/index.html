Why funny stories matter

Laughter is not a break from learning to read and listen; it is one of the best reasons to do both. A child who has laughed at a story will ask for it again, will try to read it alone, and will retell it to anyone who will listen. Funny stories also teach a quiet lesson of their own: that mistakes are survivable. The goat eats the homework, the rooster oversleeps, the umbrella is afraid of rain, and everything still turns out well.

How to read them aloud

  • Be willing to look silly. Do the voices. Squawk like the parrot. Children laugh hardest when a grown-up is plainly enjoying it too.
  • Slow down before the funny line, and pause after it. Laughter needs a little room.
  • Let the child guess what goes wrong next. Being right is delightful, and being wrong is funnier.
  • Read it again tomorrow. Children laugh more the second time, because they know what is coming.

Kind laughter

In every story here, the reader laughs with a character and at a situation, never at someone for being small, slow or different. That is worth pointing out to an older child, gently, because it marks the line between a joke and teasing. Each story also carries a small lesson underneath, and the three questions at the end bring it out without spoiling the fun. For more to laugh at, try the clean animal jokes for kids.
