What suits this age

At four and five, children want a little something to happen: a cloud looks for a bed, an owl goes round hushing the noises of the night, two children build a fort out of a blanket. But at bedtime the "something" must be small and safe. In these stories nobody is lost, chased or in danger. A character looks for rest and finds it.

How to read them

They take three to four minutes each. This is the age of "one more story", so agree on the number before you begin. Children of this age also start to imagine vividly once the light is off; a story that ends in a cosy, pictured place, such as a pillow garden or a boat on a slow river, gives the mind somewhere pleasant to go. Each story has a short note on how to read it.
