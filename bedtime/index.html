What makes a story a bedtime story

Not every children's story is good at bedtime. An exciting adventure wakes a child up; a funny one starts the giggles. The stories here were written for the last ten minutes of the day, and they follow a few plain rules:

  • Nothing scary. No one is lost, chased or in danger, even for a moment.
  • They slow down. Sentences get shorter and quieter towards the end.
  • They repeat. A line or a sound comes back again and again, like a lullaby.
  • They end in sleep. The last picture in the child's mind is of someone resting.
  • No questions afterwards. There is no moral to discuss and no quiz. The story ends and the day ends with it.

Choose by age

The stories are sorted by the age they suit: 2 to 3, 4 to 5 and 6 to 8. The ages are a guide, not a rule. Many children enjoy a younger story when they are tired, and that is exactly the right choice on such a night.

Reading from a phone at night

A bright screen in a dark room does not help anyone sleep. Three things on each story page reduce the glare. The Night button turns the page dark and dims it. The A+ button makes the letters larger, so that you can hold the phone further away. And Read aloud lets the phone read slowly in its own voice while the screen lies face down. Your own voice is better than any machine's, so use that button only on nights when you are worn out. Better still, print a story and read it by a small lamp.

For the grown-up

Every story begins with a short note for the person reading: where to slow down, which sound to repeat, when to whisper. If bedtime is hard in your house, three pages may help: a simple bedtime routine, what to do about "one more story" and other delays, and a page of short goodnight lines to say at the door. You can also make a story with your child's own name in it.

Are these stories original?

Yes. Every story was written for this site. None is taken from a book or another website, and none repeats a story from our other story sites.

How long should a bedtime story be?

Two to five minutes of reading is enough for most children under eight. One story read calmly does more than three read in a hurry.

My child wants the same story every night. Is that all right?

It is more than all right. Knowing every word is what makes a story restful. Read it again.
